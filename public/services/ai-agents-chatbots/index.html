AI agents and chatbots with clear boundaries

Start with the user and task

A useful business chatbot is more than a chat window. It needs a clear audience, an approved knowledge source, a defined scope, and an escalation path. We map those pieces before choosing a model or integration, then test the assistant against realistic questions and failure cases.

Common business assistant use cases

  • Customer support assistantAnswer recurring questions from approved help content and route unresolved issues.
  • Lead qualification chatbotAsk relevant intake questions and prepare structured information for your team.
  • Internal knowledge assistantHelp staff locate and summarize information they are permitted to access.
  • Workflow-connected agentTrigger narrowly scoped actions with permission checks and review where appropriate.

Designed for safe operation

We define what the assistant may answer, what actions it can take, which sources it can use, and when it must stop or hand off. Testing includes unknown questions, conflicting information, sensitive data, and integration errors. Ongoing evaluation should reflect real user feedback and the business impact of mistakes.

AI agent and chatbot questions

What is the difference between an AI chatbot and an AI agent?

A chatbot primarily handles conversational responses. An AI agent may also use approved tools to carry out defined steps. The distinction depends on the system’s capabilities and permissions.

Can a chatbot use our own content?

Yes, if the content can be securely accessed and maintained. Source quality, access controls, and update frequency all affect answer quality.

Can the assistant hand a conversation to a person?

Yes. Escalation can be designed for uncertain answers, sensitive topics, explicit user requests, or cases that require human judgment.
